THE BASICS

Getting started

What is InfoMancer?

InfoMancer catalogs movie and TV files already stored on your server. It helps you browse the library, match titles to metadata, identify missing episodes, and review safer Plex-friendly names.

Does InfoMancer stream or play my media?

No. InfoMancer organizes and audits the library; a media server such as Plex remains responsible for playback.

Can I replay the walkthrough?

Yes. Open your Profile menu and choose Take the Tour, or open Profile and use the walkthrough card.

ON DISK

Libraries and scans

Source management and scans are available to Librarians.

What is a source?

A source is a server folder containing Movies or TV Shows. Movie sources may contain individual files, title folders, or alphabet and number buckets. TV sources normally contain one folder per series with season folders beneath it.

What does a scan change?

A scan reads supported video files and updates InfoMancer’s catalog. Scanning does not rename, move, or delete media.

When should I rescan?

Scan after adding, removing, or moving media. Use Scan all sources for a broad refresh, or scan one source or series when you know exactly what changed.

Why can’t InfoMancer browse a folder I know exists?

The folder must be mounted on the machine running InfoMancer and visible inside its container. A path available only on your laptop is not automatically available to the server.

IDENTITY

Matching and metadata

What does matching do?

Matching connects a local title to its authoritative movie or series record. InfoMancer stores the identifier and uses it for artwork, ratings, credits, episode lists, links, and Plex-compatible names.

What if the suggested match is wrong?

Choose Fix Match or Change Match, search again, or enter the provider ID or link directly. A match is not applied until you approve it.

Why is artwork, a rating, or cast information missing?

The title may still be unmatched, may not have an IMDb identifier, or may need a metadata refresh. Use the title’s three-dot menu to refresh IMDb metadata for that movie, series, or episode.

What is the difference between scanning and refreshing metadata?

Scanning checks your files. Metadata refreshes check external title information such as genres, ratings, people, and IDs. Neither operation renames files by itself.

TV SHOWS

Episodes and known gaps

How are missing episodes calculated?

After a series is matched, InfoMancer compares its expected aired episodes with the season and episode numbers parsed from local video files. Future episodes and specials outside the normal seasons are handled separately.

Why does a show report missing episodes that I actually have?

The filename may not contain a recognizable season and episode code, the show may be matched to the wrong series, or the provider’s episode order may differ from your files. Check the match and episode naming, then rescan the series.

How do I see exactly what is missing?

Open the series and choose Missing Episodes. You can review each gap and use the configured search provider link to look for it. InfoMancer presents searches but does not download files itself.

FILESYSTEM SAFETY

Renaming and folder changes

Will InfoMancer rename files automatically?

No. Filesystem changes require an explicit preview and confirmation. Bulk pages show the proposed changes before anything is applied.

What does “No changes needed” mean?

The current name already matches the proposed naming format, so InfoMancer has nothing to apply.

Can I restore an older filename?

When InfoMancer has recorded the original imported name, use Restore Original Filenames from the series actions. Always review the restoration preview first.

ACCESS

Accounts and roles

What is the difference between a Member and a Librarian?

Members can browse and search the catalog and manage their own account. Librarians can also configure sources, scan libraries, match titles, refresh metadata, approve file changes, manage users, and publish announcements.

Where can I change my profile or password?

Open the Profile button in the upper-right corner. Profile, Password, and Sessions let you update your account and sign out other browsers.

What if I forget my local-account password?

If another Librarian can sign in, they can open Profile → Users and create a single-use password-reset link. If every Librarian is locked out, someone with terminal access to the InfoMancer server can generate one:

docker compose exec infomancer python -m app.cli recovery-link USERNAME --base-url https://your-infomancer-address

The link expires after one hour by default, works once, and signs out the account’s existing sessions after the new password is saved. Never share a recovery link publicly.

Why was I signed out?

Your session may have expired, been revoked, or been invalidated by a password change. Sign in again; if that fails, ask a Librarian to check your account.

YOUR VIEW

Favorites, tags, and sorting

Who can see my favorites, ratings, and tags?

They belong to your account. They help organize the shared catalog without changing another person’s view or any media file.

How do I tag several titles together?

Select movies or TV series in List view, then choose Organize & Tags. Existing and newly created tags can be added to the whole selection.

Why is runtime, resolution, or HDR information missing?

A Librarian must run Media Inspection from System Settings or the title’s three-dot menu. InfoMancer reads the file with FFprobe and does not modify it.

TAKE YOUR DATA WITH YOU

Library exports

Exports are read-only snapshots. Creating one does not modify the catalog or any media file.

Which export format should I choose?

CSV is usually best for spreadsheets. Open it in Excel, Numbers, LibreOffice Calc, or a data-analysis tool to sort, filter, chart, and audit your library.

JSON is usually best for backups, scripts, and moving structured data between applications. It preserves field names and nested export information cleanly.

XML is useful when another application, automation system, or archival workflow specifically accepts XML. It is structured and widely supported, but less convenient for manual spreadsheet work.

What information is included?

Each format contains the same catalog snapshot: titles, kinds, years, source and file paths, provider IDs, ratings, genres, tags, collections, custom fields, file sizes, episode numbers, and any collected runtime, resolution, codec, bitrate, container, and HDR/SDR details.

Is a library export a complete InfoMancer backup?

No. It is a portable record for reference, analysis, or integration. It does not contain accounts, passwords, sessions, application settings, provider credentials, artwork files, or the media itself. Use a database and configuration backup when you need to restore an entire installation.

WHEN SOMETHING LOOKS WRONG

Troubleshooting

A background task seems stuck. What should I do?

Open the task widget to read its current status. Large scans and metadata imports can take time. If the status never changes, refresh the page and ask a Librarian to check the System page and application logs.

Why does a recent interface change not appear?

Your browser may still have an older stylesheet or script cached. Perform a forced refresh or open a fresh private window before reporting the issue.

What information helps a Librarian diagnose a problem?

Include the page you were using, the title or source involved, what you clicked, what you expected, the exact plain-language error shown, and a screenshot when possible. Do not share passwords, API keys, tunnel tokens, or activation links.

Where can a Librarian download logs or catalog data?

Open App Settings and choose System. The troubleshooting area opens the live log and exports it, while Library Data downloads the catalog as CSV, JSON, or XML.
